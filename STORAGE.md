# Storage Layout — append-only log

The population goes live on **2026-09-02** and must never stop. From the moment of that
deploy, `Prophet` sits behind a beacon and `Population` behind a UUPS proxy specifically
so **logic can be repaired without resetting lineage** — the ancestry graph is the one
asset in this project that cannot be rebuilt in a hurry.

> The deploy was **moved from 2026-08-30 to 2026-09-02** on 2026-08-29, precisely so the
> storage-affecting half of the arena-engine rework lands before the freeze rather than
> after it. **All of it now has: phases 1, 2, 2A, 3 and 4 are in**, and phase 4 — the
> escalating ante, seasons, prize pool and rake — was the last one that could move a slot.
> It took the four it was predicted to take. Moving the deploy cost no generations,
> because the run's length is capped by the STT budget rather than by its start date.
> Phase 5 (a second venue adapter) was predicted here to be logic and calldata only, and
> **it landed on 2026-08-30 and the prediction held** — it added one new file and touched
> neither proxied contract. Confirmed against the compiler rather than against the diff;
> see the last changelog entry.

That guarantee holds only if storage never moves.

## The rule

**Append only, into `__gap`.** Never reorder a variable, never change a type, never
delete one, never insert between existing ones. Shrinking `__gap` by exactly the
number of slots you add is the only permitted edit to it. Every change gets a dated
entry in the changelog at the bottom, including changes made *before* the freeze.

Renaming a variable is safe (names are not on-chain). Changing `public` to
`internal` is safe for storage but breaks the frontend, so treat it as a breaking
change anyway.

**Slot 14 is exactly full.** A new packed counter cannot join it; it must start a new
slot out of `__gap`.

**Slots 0 and 15 have free bytes — do not fill them either.** Slot 0 is 31/32 (one
byte spare) and **slot 15 is 1/32 (thirty-one bytes spare)**, which makes it by far the
most inviting place in this contract to "just add a bool". It is the same trap as the
`Population` slots below: packing a new variable into a partially-used slot changes
nothing for a fresh deploy and corrupts nothing visibly until a beacon upgrade puts a
live organism's counter on top of someone else's bytes. Every addition takes a fresh
slot out of `__gap`, without exception.

**`__slotAlign` at slot 16 is padding, and it is why `entrant` is at 17 rather than
packed into slot 15.** Declaration order alone would have put `address entrant` at slot
15 offset 1, silently consuming twenty of the thirty-one bytes the note above tells you
not to fill — declaring it after `positionOpen` is exactly the trap, and the trap works
on the person adding the field too. A `uint256` cannot pack into anything by definition,
so one wasted slot forces the boundary and buys a clean, obviously-safe layout for the
next person who adds a field. Mirrors `Population.__slotAlign` at slot 27, for the same
reason and at the same cost. Compiler-verified: `entrant` reports slot 17 offset 0, and
`positionOpen` is still alone in 15.

### Why `currentStake` and `currentQuantity` are both here

A paired mint (`mintSet(yesTo, noTo, amount)`) is funded by *both* organisms, so each
side holds `amount` outcome tokens while having risked only its own contribution
(≈`amount / 2`). Redemption is denominated in tokens; profit and loss in collateral.
Storing one number and deriving the other would make **every winner read as a
break-even**, silently zeroing the fitness signal that the entire project rests on.

Slots 33 and 39 are the two places in this contract where packing was *chosen*
rather than avoided, and the distinction is the whole point of the rule. Slot 33's
seven fields are **one concept** — a season's climate — declared together in a single
group in one commit, so no boundary inside the slot has ever been the edge of a
shipped layout; 26 of 32 bytes are used and the remaining six are covered by the
do-not-fill rule below. Slot 39 is the same argument at smaller scale: `windowVenue`
and `windowRakeBps` were appended together on 2026-09-06, are written by adjacent
statements in `think()` and read by adjacent statements in `settleAll()`, and neither
had ever been deployed, so the slot was theirs from the start. 22 of 32 bytes used,
ten spare and do-not-fill.

Both are categorically different from appending `venue` into `phase`'s spare bytes,
where the slot was **already frozen**. The test is not "is the slot partially full"
but "was any boundary inside it already the edge of a shipped layout".

`__slotAlign` is padding and it is load-bearing. `phase` at slot 26 is a `uint8`,
so its slot has thirty-one bytes spare, and `address public venue` declared after
it would have landed at **slot 26 offset 1** — the packing this document and
`CLAUDE.md` both forbid. A `uint256` cannot fit in thirty-one bytes, so declaring
one is what forces `venue` onto a slot of its own; there is no padding primitive
that does it more directly. The compiler confirms the outcome: `phase` is alone in
slot 26 and `venue` is alone at slot 28 offset 0. Cost: two slots out of twenty,
one of them never read, in exchange for every pre-existing boundary staying where
this table says it is.

Slots 13, 18, 21, 23, 26, 33, 38 and 39 all have free bytes. **Do not fill them** — packing a new
variable into a partially-used slot is exactly the edit that looks safe and is not,
because it changes nothing for a fresh deploy and corrupts nothing visibly until an
organism's counter starts reading someone else's bytes. Take a fresh slot from
`__gap`.

## Deliberately NOT in Prophet

Every economic parameter — `endowment`, `metabolicCost`, `minStake`, `stakeBps`,
`breedStreak`, `breedSurplusBps` — lives in `Population` and is passed in per call.
The whole ten-day run can therefore be recalibrated from a single transaction without
a beacon upgrade and without touching organism storage. If death turns out to be too
slow or too fast to film, that is a `setEconomics` call, not a migration.

## Contracts with no storage constraint

`SelectionEngine`, `PushedPriceSource`, `DreamDEXVenue` and `DirectDuelVenue` are all
plain, non-upgradeable contracts. None of them has an append-only obligation: there is
no proxy in front of any of them, so a redeploy starts from a blank layout and the four
tables above do not apply. That is intentional — they wrap the parts of the integration
that were still unverified (the reactivity callback selector, the price read) or that are
meant to be swappable by design (adjudication), so the risky surfaces are the replaceable
ones. Their layouts, for the record rather than as a constraint: `SelectionEngine` and
`PushedPriceSource` as before, `DreamDEXVenue` is `symbol` 0 / `poolOf` 1, and
`DirectDuelVenue` is `symbol` 0 / `duelCount` 1 / `duels` 2.

**But "no storage constraint" is not "re-pointable at any moment", and for one of the four
that distinction can lose money silently.** `Population.setWiring` has no phase guard, so
the safe window is a property of the operator's timing rather than of the code — which is
why the test that covers it is named `test_venue_canBeRepointedBetweenWindows` and calls
`_settle()` before it repoints. **Repoint the venue in phase 0, with no position open.**

What goes wrong otherwise is worth spelling out, because the two adapters fail in opposite
directions and only one of them is loud:

- **`DreamDEXVenue` reverts.** `redeemFor` on an id it never issued hits
  `UnknownPosition(positionId)` (`DreamDEXVenue.sol:98`), which
  `test_venue_rejectsAPositionItNeverIssued` asserts by selector *and* argument. `settleAll`
  isolates it as `SettleFailed` for that organism, so the failure is visible and the position
  is still open. That test's own docblock names the alternative it exists to prevent — *"a
  silent zero payout rather than a revert"* — which is the next bullet exactly.
- **`DirectDuelVenue` returns zero.** `redeemFor` on a duel it does not know returns `0` at
  `DirectDuelVenue.sol:220` — correctly, because `IArenaVenue` *requires* a loser to be paid
  0 rather than revert, and a fresh venue cannot tell "not mine" from "lost". So the window
  completes, both duellists are graded as total losses, and the escrowed backing stays in the
  abandoned venue. There is no path out: `redeemFor` reverts `NotHolder` for anyone but the
  organism itself, `Prophet` only ever calls the venue Population currently names, and
  `settleWindow` opens with `if (!positionOpen) revert NothingCommitted()` — so repointing
  back does not let the settlement be retried. Recovering that collateral would need a beacon
  upgrade.

This is a consequence of `DirectDuelVenue` **holding value between transactions**, which
`DreamDEXVenue` does not: a duel's backing sits in the venue from `openOpposing` until both
sides redeem, whereas complete sets are pushed through in one call and
`test_venue_organismPushesRatherThanTheVenuePulling` asserts the DreamDEX adapter ends the
window holding zero outcome tokens *and* zero collateral. A venue that escrows is a venue you
cannot walk away from mid-window.
